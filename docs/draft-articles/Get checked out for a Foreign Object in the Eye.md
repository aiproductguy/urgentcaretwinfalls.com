# Get Checked Out for a Foreign Object in the Eye

Anyone who has had the misfortune of getting something stuck in their eye knows all too well the discomfort and severe pain it can cause.

From dirt, hair, or more significant objects, the eyes are susceptible to external injury. Outdoor activities, work-related incidents, and even a windy day that sweeps up debris can create conditions that make eye protection important. And sometimes even with safety glasses injury from foreign objects entering the eye cannot be prevented.

## Common Types of Foreign Objects

Common objects that can get into your eyes include:

- **Dirt**: Small particles carried by the wind can easily find their way into the eye, especially in dry, open areas or during windy conditions.
- **Metal Shavings/Wood Particles**: People using a grinder or construction industries are at risk of having tiny metal fragments enter their eyes, which can be particularly dangerous. Sawdust and wood are also common in carpentry and construction.
- **Eyelashes**: An eyelash falling into the eye is a common minor irritation.
- **Insects**: Small flying insects can enter the eye causing discomfort and irritation.
- **Sand**: Beachgoers often experience sand getting into their eyes, which can scratch the cornea.
- **Chemical Splashes**: Chemicals splashing into the eye constitute a serious emergency, requiring immediate flushing and medical attention.
- **Makeup Particles**: Particles from mascara, eyeshadow, or other makeup products can get into the eye, leading to irritation.
- **Contact Lens Pieces**: A broken contact lens can leave small fragments in the eye, causing discomfort and potential injury.

These objects vary in severity from minor irritants that can be washed out to more serious hazards requiring professional medical attention. It's crucial to treat all foreign objects in the eye with care to prevent damage to the delicate structures of your eye.

## Should I Go to Urgent Care for a Foreign Object in the Eye? The Red Flags

Not every foreign object in the eye necessitates a visit to urgent care. Sometimes, simple irrigation at home is enough to flush out the irritant. However, certain red flags suggest that professional medical attention is necessary:

- **Persistent Pain or Discomfort**: If after attempting to rinse your eye, discomfort or pain persists, it could indicate that the object is still present or has caused damage.
- **Impaired Vision**: Any loss of vision or significant visual disturbance following an eye injury is a clear indicator that medical care is required.
- **Visible Object**: If you can see the object in your eye and it does not come out with gentle rinsing, do not attempt to remove it yourself. Seek professional care.
- **Excessive Redness or Tearing**: While some irritation is expected with foreign objects in the eye, excessive redness or tearing that does not improve may suggest a more serious issue.
