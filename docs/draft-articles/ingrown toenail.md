An ingrown toenail occurs when the corner of the toenail grows into the surrounding skin, often causing pain, redness, and swelling. It commonly affects the big toe and can be triggered by improper nail trimming, tight shoes, or trauma. While it usually isn't serious, it can lead to infection, especially if left untreated. People with diabetes, poor circulation, or nerve damage are at higher risk. Treatment involves soaking the foot, gently lifting the nail, and using antibiotic creams. In severe cases, surgery may be needed to remove part of the nail. Preventive measures include cutting nails straight across and wearing well-fitting shoes. Most cases heal with proper care, but persistent or infected ingrown toenails require medical attention.
